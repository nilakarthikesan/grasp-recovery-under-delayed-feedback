# System Design: Latency-Conditioned Intervention Value for Grasp Recovery

**Status:** implemented pilot (simulation). Numbers quoted in the paper come
from `results/pilot_v1/report.json`, regenerated by the commands in the README.

## 1. Thesis

> The value of a grasp-recovery action is a joint function of the physical
> state, the remedy, and the age of the evidence when the remedy takes effect
> (observation age + response delay). Each remedy therefore has its own
> deadline, and the deadlines shift with response delay. A supervisor that
> conditions its intervention-value estimate on its own latency chooses
> different remedies and different firing times than a latency-agnostic
> supervisor or a failure detector driving a fixed remedy; whether that
> translates into more completed tasks at a matched intervention budget is an
> empirical question that the pilot answers in the negative for this task.

Three claims are tested, in order of strength (pilot verdicts in §11):

1. **Remedy-specific deadlines exist.** From privileged matched branches, each
   remedy's recoverability as a function of lead time before nominal loss has a
   different shape; delay shifts these curves differently per remedy
   (Fig. frontier). This is a property of the task and the remedy menu, not of
   any learner.
2. **Latency-conditioning changes decisions.** Trained on the same branch
   labels and the same stale observations, the delay-aware value selector
   shifts its remedy mix toward delay-tolerant remedies as delay grows and
   changes its firing threshold; the delay-agnostic selector cannot.
3. **It improves completion at a matched budget.** Against reactive slip
   detection + fixed remedy, future-loss prediction + fixed remedy, a
   delay-agnostic value selector, and random interventions at matched budget,
   with paired bootstrap confidence intervals over held-out parent episodes.
   (Not supported in the pilot; see §11.)

Feedback-null controls (masked, episode-mismatched, frozen observations)
establish that the selector's gain depends on informative observations rather
than on timing features or episode statistics.

## 2. What is new relative to the closest work

The project does not claim a new failure detector, a new latency benchmark, or
a new recovery policy class. The candidate contribution is the **measured
interaction** of observation age, response delay, remedy choice, and hidden
contact physics, from matched states with persistent task outcomes, plus the
**latency-conditioned intervention-value model** as a hybrid artifact. The
closest overlaps and their boundaries are listed in
[GRASP_RECOVERY_UNDER_DELAYED_FEEDBACK.md](GRASP_RECOVERY_UNDER_DELAYED_FEEDBACK.md).

Relation to the separate `grasp-failure-recovery` project: that project asks
whether pre-lift signals predict eventual outcome for dexterous HUG grasps.
This project asks the complementary question: once an outcome is at risk,
how does the value of each corrective action decay with the age of the
evidence, and can a supervisor exploit that? No code, assets, or data are
shared; the evaluation/logging discipline (versioned protocol, privileged vs
deployable separation, grouped splits, bitwise replay checks) is deliberately
parallel so the two papers can be read together.

## 3. Task and simulator

* MuJoCo 3.16, `src/delayed_recovery/assets/gripper_transport.xml`.
* Cartesian carrier (x, z prismatic joints, kp = 2500 position servos,
  gravity-compensated own mass) with a parallel-jaw gripper whose fingers are
  force-controlled motors (ctrl range −3 to 7 N; the bound is deliberately
  tight so that squeezing is not a free fix). This models an ideal
  arm-level position controller and a force-limited gripper.
* 4 cm rigid cube; **hidden** mass U(0.15, 0.50) kg (inertia updated
  consistently), sliding friction U(0.35, 0.80) (object geom dominates every
  contact because pads/table use 0.05; the effective coefficient is read back
  from `data.contact` every step and asserted in tests), grasp x-offset
  U(−8, 8) mm, and an exogenous **push**: an external force on the object
  equal to its mass × a_p, a_p ~ U(0, 8) m/s², direction (0.6, 0, −0.8), for
  4 control steps (200 ms), starting 1–10 steps after transport begins. The
  push is part of the hidden condition and is identical in every branch of an
  episode, so matched branches stay matched.
* Nominal plan (known to the controller): transport speed {0.8, 1.2} m/s,
  acceleration {5, 8, 12} m/s². Approach → close (4 N) → lift 10 cm → transport
  0.45 m → lower (support contact detected from the wrist load) → release →
  retreat → 1 s dwell.
* The grasp line is 3 cm above the cube centre, so lateral acceleration makes
  a pendulum torque that the friction patch must resist. Failures therefore
  develop gradually (tilt + descent in hand) over 50–950 ms (median 350 ms
  from a 3 mm in-hand descent to loss), which creates a recovery window.
  77 of the 100 nominal losses in the pilot begin at or after the push.
* 20 Hz control; 2 ms physics; carrier setpoints are first-order-held across
  substeps (as a low-level interpolator would) because zero-order hold produced
  servo chatter that dominated the physics.
* Completion = `placed`: inside a 3 cm box around the target, tilt < 15°,
  speed < 2 cm/s after the dwell. Other labels: `lost`, `misplaced`,
  `preserved_abort`, `acquisition_failed`, `timeout`.

## 4. Observation contract

Deployable (selector inputs): carrier joint pos/vel, finger joint pos/vel,
finger actuator force, wrist force/torque (6), last command (4) — 20 values
per step — plus derived finger gap, steps since acquisition, controller phase,
the planned transport speed/acceleration, and the change of wrist load / gap
since the lift reference. Feature window: 4 steps ending at the observation
time. Timing features: observation age, response delay, their sum.

Privileged (labels, oracles, stratification only): object pose/velocity,
relative descent in hand, tilt, contact count, effective friction, hidden mass.
RGB is not used in the pilot; this is a declared narrowing of the proposal.

## 5. Timing contract

Per decision at parent step *t*: observation captured at *t − a*
(observation age *a* steps), decision at *t*, remedy requested at *t*, remedy
effective at *t + d* (response delay *d* steps). Nominal continuation executes
between *t* and *t + d* (queue-preserving hold policy: the controller keeps
following its committed plan). Physics never pauses. Grid: *a* ∈ {0, 2, 5}
steps = {0, 100, 250} ms; *d* ∈ {0, 2, 5, 10} = {0, 100, 250, 500} ms.

Observation age affects only what the selector sees; response delay affects
only the physics. Hence branch outcomes are indexed by (state, remedy, *d*),
features by (state, *a*).

## 6. Matched branching and exact closed-loop evaluation

At every control step in the lift and transport phases of an acquired parent
episode, the complete system (MuJoCo `mjSTATE_INTEGRATION` incl. warm-start,
latched observation, controller phase machine, plan, counters) is saved.
Restoring a snapshot and continuing reproduces the parent bitwise
(`tests/test_env_and_replay.py`). Each remedy × delay branch is run to its
persistent outcome.

Because a supervisor cannot alter the world before it fires and one
intervention is allowed per episode, a supervisor's closed-loop outcome at
(*a*, *d*) equals the branch outcome of the first state at which it fires.
`scripts/verify_lookup.py` re-simulates sampled episodes with the learned
supervisor in the loop and confirms identical outcomes, firing steps and
remedies.

## 7. Remedy menu (deployable inputs only)

| remedy | action | expected sensitivity |
|---|---|---|
| continue | none | — |
| slow | scale remaining speed/acceleration ×0.4 | useful only while acceleration remains ahead |
| squeeze | finger force 4 → 7 N (actuator bound) | fast, most delay-tolerant |
| place/regrasp | decelerate, lower to support contact, open, re-close at 7 N, re-lift, resume at ×0.5 | slow, needs the object still in hand; can abort |

## 8. Selectors

* nominal; privileged menu oracle (upper bound); random at the realised budget.
* reactive slip detector + squeeze (trained on privileged *current* slip label
  at the observation time).
* future-loss predictor + squeeze (trained on loss under continuation).
* value selector, delay-agnostic: per-remedy completion models on stale
  features only, trained on the full timing mixture.
* value selector, delay-aware: same plus (age, delay, evidence age) inputs.
  Fires argmax remedy when estimated value over continuation ≥ τ.

All learned components are gradient-boosted trees (scikit-learn) fitted on the
training split; τ (or detector threshold) is selected on validation to maximise
pooled completion subject to an intervention budget (≤ 40 % of episodes;
the natural loss prevalence among acquired episodes is 32 %). Test episodes
are untouched until the end. A second operating-point variant (`@celltuned`)
chooses one threshold per timing cell on validation, so the model/operating
point ablation is 2 × 2: {delay-agnostic, delay-aware} × {pooled, per-cell}.

## 9. Splits and statistics

Episode-level split 50/20/30 of acquired parent episodes; all decisions,
ages and branches of an episode stay together. A secondary held-out friction
band (0.50–0.58) checks interpolation to unseen physics. Uncertainty: paired
bootstrap over parent episodes (2000 resamples); cells are not independent.
Metrics: completion, paired gain vs nominal, intervention rate, rescue rate,
harm rate (interventions that turned a nominal success into a non-success),
unnecessary rate, median lead time.

## 10. Limits

Simulation only; one object geometry; ideal Cartesian carrier; deterministic
physics (paired outcomes, not calibrated probabilities); fixed synthetic
delays rather than measured latency distributions; no RGB; the remedy menu and
the controller are scripted; a single push disturbance; 94 test episodes, so
differences below about two completion points are unresolved. These bound
every claim.

## 11. Pilot findings (`results/pilot_v1/report.json`)

400 episodes, 314 acquired, 100 lost nominally; 7,394 decision states,
88,728 branches; split 157/63/94; 60/60 live re-simulations match the lookup.

* **Claim 1 supported.** 50 %-recovery deadlines (decision lead before nominal
  loss) at delays 0/100/250/500 ms: squeeze 50/150/300/550 ms, place/regrasp
  350/450/600/850 ms; slow never exceeds 33 % of lost states. The privileged
  menu oracle completes 100 % of test episodes at every delay while
  intervening in 32 %, so every nominal loss was physically recoverable.
* **Claim 2 supported.** The delay-aware selector lowers its intervention
  rate with delay (0.48 → 0.37) and shifts toward place/regrasp with 250 ms-old
  evidence (22 of 44 interventions at zero delay vs 1 of 41 for the agnostic
  selector at fresh evidence). Reactive slip detection + squeeze collapses
  from 1.00 to 0.72 across the grid; anticipatory supervisors span 0.99 → 0.87.
* **Claim 3 not supported.** Pooled test completion: nominal 0.681, random
  0.739, reactive 0.852, future-loss + squeeze 0.946, value delay-agnostic
  0.934, value delay-aware 0.930, oracle 1.000. Aware − agnostic
  −0.4 pt [−1.0, +0.3]; aware − future-loss −1.6 pt [−2.4, −0.9]. Per-cell
  thresholds do not change the ordering. Held-out friction band 0.50–0.58
  (n = 64): nominal 0.562, future-loss 0.814, agnostic 0.829, aware 0.812,
  oracle 1.000.
* **Feedback nulls** (delay-aware selector, test): frozen 0.810
  (−12.0 pt), mismatched 0.838 (−9.2), masked 0.894 (−3.6, degenerate: fires in
  all or no episodes of a cell depending only on timing inputs).
* **Inference** on one Apple M4 core: detectors 2.9–3.3 ms median, value
  selectors 11.6 ms median (p95 22 ms); all inside one 50 ms control step.

Interpretation for the paper: the measurable contribution is the protocol,
the remedy-specific deadlines, the reactive-vs-anticipatory divergence under
evidence age, and the oracle recoverability result; the latency-conditioned
value model is reported as an honest negative within this task and grid, with
the conditions (fragile objects, weaker squeeze, measured latency traces)
under which it is expected to matter stated as the next experiment.
